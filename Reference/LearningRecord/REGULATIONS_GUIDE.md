## SERIES 1 — SOLAS CH. II-1

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Badge ID** | `solas_ii1` |
| **Title** | SOLAS Chapter II-1 — Construction, Subdivision & Stability |
| **Category** | Regulations |
| **Description** | SOLAS Chapter II-1 governs the structural design, subdivision, and stability of ships. It establishes how ships must be divided into watertight compartments and defines the damage stability criteria they must meet. The chapter has been significantly strengthened by the 2009 SOLAS amendments, introducing probabilistic damage stability for all passenger and cargo ships. It also covers machinery and electrical installation requirements, including emergency power and steering gear. |

> Context: How Chapter II-1 protects ships from flooding through structural design and watertight integrity.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|

This episode covers what the chapter applies to and the key 2009 SOLAS amendments that modernized damage stability requirements. |
| 2 | Subdivision and the Floodable Length | The "floodable length" concept defines how many adjacent compartments can flood and the ship still survive. This episode covers the factor of subdivision, how it varies with vessel type and service, and the risk of progressive flooding via open watertight doors and unclosed manholes. |

## SERIES 4 — SOLAS CH. IV

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Badge ID** | `solas_iv` |
| **Title** | SOLAS Chapter IV — Radio Communications (GMDSS) |
| **Category** | Regulations |
| **Description** | SOLAS Chapter IV implements the Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS), replacing the old Morse code watchkeeping system with a zone-based satellite and terrestrial communications framework. Equipment requirements scale with the sea area in which the ship operates, from A1 (coastal VHF range) to A4 (polar regions not covered by satellite). The chapter also governs GMDSS maintenance methods and the certification of radio operators. |
| **Background image** | GMDSS radio console on bridge |
| **Card thumbnail** | Radio/satellite communication icon |

---

### Season 1 — GMDSS Architecture (order: 1)
> Context: How GMDSS divides the world's oceans into sea areas and what equipment each zone requires.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chapter IV and GMDSS | Chapter IV implements GMDSS, which covers all ships over 300 GT on international voyages and passenger ships on any voyage. This episode explains the four sea areas — A1 (VHF coast station range), A2 (MF), A3 (Inmarsat satellite coverage), and A4 (polar) — and the principle that equipment requirements scale with the distance from shore and the available communication systems. |
| 2 | Required Equipment by Sea Area | A1 ships require VHF DSC and a float-free EPIRB. A1+A2 ships add MF DSC and NAVTEX. A3 ships require either Inmarsat or MF/HF equipment. A4 ships require MF/HF. This episode also covers the two-way radio requirement for survival craft and the choice between SART and AIS-SART. |
| 3 | GMDSS Maintenance Options | Chapter IV allows three maintenance methods: Method 1 (at least one qualified radio technician on board), Method 2 (duplication of all GMDSS equipment), and Method 3 (a shore-based maintenance agreement with a certified service provider). This episode explains why most commercial ships opt for Method 3 and what that agreement must cover. |

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distress Communications | A DSC distress alert is transmitted on Channel 70 (VHF) or 2187.5 kHz (MF/HF) and automatically includes the ship's MMSI number and position. ### Season 1 — Bridge Equipment & Standards (order: 1)
> Context: The equipment Chapter V mandates on the bridge and why it applies universally.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chapter V — Universal Applicability | Unlike other SOLAS chapters, Chapter V applies to all ships on all voyages, including non-SOLAS vessels such as small yachts on ocean passages. This episode explains the scope of application, the voyage planning obligation under Regulation 34, and why the chapter's universal reach makes it the most PSC-scrutinized SOLAS chapter. |
| 2 | Mandatory Bridge Equipment | Ships of 300 GT and above on international voyages must carry at least one radar; ships of 10,000 GT and above must carry ARPA. AIS is required for ships of 300 GT and above. ECDIS is mandated as an alternative to paper charts for new ships from 2012 onward. This episode explains each equipment type, its function, and the carriage thresholds. |
| 3 | AIS Obligations | AIS transmits both static data (vessel name, MMSI, call sign, vessel type, dimensions) and dynamic data (position, speed over ground, course over ground, rate of turn). | 4 | VDR and S-VDR | The Voyage Data Recorder stores bridge audio, radar display, AIS data, rudder and engine orders, and GMDSS communications on a continuous 12-hour loop. This episode covers S-VDR as the simplified alternative for existing ships, the annual performance test requirement, and how VDR data is used in post-accident investigations. |

| **Title** | STCW Regulation II — Deck Officers |
| **Category** | Regulations |
| **Description** | STCW Chapter II establishes the certification and competency standards for all deck officers, from the Officer of the Watch at operational level through to the Chief Mate and Master at management level. It defines the minimum sea service, the approved training pathways, and the revalidation requirements that keep deck officer certificates in force. PSC enforces these standards directly by verifying seafarer certificates and testing crew knowledge during inspections. |

### Season 1 — Deck Watch Officers (order: 1)
> Context: The certification requirements for OOWs and how STCW Reg. II/1 is applied in practice.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regulation II/1 — OOW Navigation | Competency table A-II/1 sets out 7 functions and 24 competencies an OOW must demonstrate for certification. This episode explains the competency-based assessment framework, the practical and oral examination requirements, and the minimum sea service of 12 months (with at least 6 months on bridge watchkeeping duties) required before certification. |
| 2 | The Certificate of Competency for OOW | Flag states — such as Panama's AMP — issue the CoC after verifying training and sea service. | 3 | Competency Functions for OOW | The 7 functions at operational level include navigation, cargo handling and stowage, controlling ship operations, and maintenance. | 4 | Bridge Watch Duties | The COLREGS and STCW Section VIII/2 together define the watchkeeping obligations of the OOW. This episode covers the lookout requirement, who qualifies as a proper lookout, rest hour implications for the bridge team, and the growing "two persons on the bridge at night" guidance that many company SMS documents now include. |

## SERIES 18 — MOU / PSC

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Badge ID** | `mou_psc` |
| **Title** | MOU and Port State Control |
| **Category** | Regulations |
| **Description** | Port State Control is the primary enforcement mechanism for international shipping conventions, allowing coastal states to inspect foreign-flagged vessels in their ports. The nine regional Memoranda of Understanding coordinate PSC across the world, share inspection data, and publish targeting criteria that determine which ships face the highest inspection frequency. Understanding how PSC works — from ship selection through to detention and appeal — is essential knowledge for every seafarer and ship manager. |
| **Background image** | PSC inspector boarding vessel |
| **Card thumbnail** | PSC badge / inspection checklist |

---

### Season 1 — The MOU System (order: 1)
> Context: How the regional MOUs create a globally coordinated PSC enforcement network.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What are the MOUs | The nine regional MOUs are: Paris (European/North Atlantic), Tokyo (Asia-Pacific), Indian Ocean, Mediterranean, Latin America (Viña del Mar), Caribbean, Black Sea, Abuja (West and Central Africa), and Riyadh (Gulf). Each MOU covers a geographic region, all share inspection data through the Concentrated Inspection Campaign (CIC) mechanism, and all feed into the Equasis database. |
| 2 | The Paris and Tokyo MOUs | The Paris MOU (27 European and North Atlantic member states) and the Tokyo MOU (Asia-Pacific) are the two most influential regimes. This episode explains the Paris MOU white/grey/black list and how flag state classification affects the targeting of all ships flying that flag, how the Tokyo MOU targeting criteria differ, and how Equasis aggregates MOU inspection records for global access. |
| 3 | Targeting and Ship Selection | PSC authorities select ships for inspection based on risk factors: age, flag state classification, company performance history, previous detention record, ship type, cargo, and time since last inspection. This episode explains the Priority I and Priority II ship categories, how the risk score is calculated, and what ship managers can do to reduce their vessel's targeting profile. |

---

### Season 2 — The Inspection Process (order: 2)
> Context: What happens during a PSC inspection, from initial boarding to potential detention and appeal.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Initial Inspection | The first hour of a PSC inspection focuses on three areas: certificate and documentation validity (are all statutory certificates on board, current, and authentic?), the general condition of the ship, and crew familiarization testing (can crew demonstrate knowledge of fire and abandon ship procedures?). This episode explains what triggers a more detailed inspection and what a well-prepared ship looks like to an arriving PSC officer. |
| 2 | More Detailed Inspection | A more detailed inspection is triggered by clear grounds: visible deficiencies, expired certificates, poor ship condition, or evidence of non-compliance. This episode covers the areas PSC most frequently examines in detail: the Oil Record Book, Garbage Record Book, fire and abandon ship drill logs, LSA maintenance records, and Safety Management System documentation. |
| 3 | Deficiencies and Detention | PSC can issue a memo (informational note), a deficiency to be rectified by a specified date or at the next port, or a detention order preventing the ship from sailing. This episode covers the most common grounds for detention — non-functioning safety systems, certificate deficiencies, ISM failures, MARPOL violations — the notifications that must be made after detention, and the formal appeal process available to the shipowner. |
| 4 | Preparing for PSC | Effective PSC preparation involves a pre-arrival checklist, conducting a drill within 24 hours of arrival if more than 25% of crew are new joiners, organizing the certificate folder, and ensuring ORB, GRB, and drill records are complete and current. This episode explains the master's pre-arrival briefing of crew, the key questions PSC officers ask during crew familiarization testing, and how a calm, organized response to a PSC inspector reduces the risk of escalation to a more detailed inspection. |
