| **Description** | The cornerstone of maritime safety law. SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea) is the most important IMO treaty, setting minimum safety standards for the construction, equipment, and operation of merchant ships. First adopted in 1914 after the Titanic disaster, it has been continuously updated and is enforced through Flag State Control and Port State Control inspections worldwide. |

### Season 1 — Overview & History (order: 1)
> Context: Why SOLAS exists, who enforces it, and how it is structured. Essential first watch before any other SOLAS content.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is SOLAS and Why It Exists | The Titanic, the first SOLAS Convention (1914), and how maritime disasters drive international law. How IMO maintains and amends SOLAS today. |

### Season 2 — Chapter II: Construction & Fire Safety (order: 2)

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Subdivision and Damage Stability | Watertight subdivision, damage stability calculations, and why the ship must survive flooding of defined compartments. The concept of the "s-factor" and subdivision index. |

| **Category** | Codes |
| **Description** | The ISM Code (SOLAS Chapter IX) requires every shipping company and ship to have a certified Safety Management System (SMS). Introduced after the Herald of Free Enterprise and Estonia disasters, it shifted responsibility from the ship to the company, creating accountability at the management level for the first time. Understanding the ISM Code is mandatory knowledge for officers and essential context for all seafarers. |

This series covers the theory and the practical habits that prevent accidents. |

### Season 1 — Foundations of BRM (order: 1)
> Context: Where BRM comes from, what it is, and why it exists. The aviation connection.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is BRM and Where It Comes From | BRM was adapted from Crew Resource Management (CRM) in aviation after the Tenerife disaster (1977). How the same thinking was applied to ships after the Exxon Valdez (1989) and the Herald of Free Enterprise (1987). |

> Context: SA is the core cognitive skill of BRM. Loss of SA is cited in the majority of maritime collision and grounding investigations.

| # | Title | Description |

| 4 | Maintaining SA Under Fatigue | How fatigue degrades SA first before degrading motor skills. The STCW rest hours minimum and why violations are common. Practical countermeasures on watch. |

## SERIES 4 — STCW

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Badge ID** | `stcw` |
| **Title** | STCW — Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping |
| **Category** | Conventions |
| **Description** | STCW is the international convention that sets minimum qualification standards for seafarers worldwide. Adopted in 1978 and substantially revised in 1995 and 2010 (Manila Amendments), it defines what every officer and rating must know, be able to do, and be certified in before they can serve on an internationally trading vessel. Understanding STCW is understanding the legal basis for your certificate and your professional obligations. |
| **Background image** | Certificate of Competency document / ceremonial uniform officer |
| **Card thumbnail** | STCW logo / graduation cap + anchor |
| **Tags** | All fleets |

---

### Season 1 — The Convention (order: 1)
> Context: What STCW is, how it is structured, and how it was updated. The foundation for everything else.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | STCW 1978 — Origins and Purpose | Why STCW was created. Substandard ships, substandard officers. How the IMO brought 160+ nations to agree on a common standard for the first time. |
| 2 | Convention vs. Code — Part A and Part B | The "Convention" sets the framework. The "Code" Part A is mandatory (minimum standards). Part B is recommended guidance. How they interact and which one gets you detained. |
| 3 | The Manila Amendments 2010 | The biggest revision since 1995. New requirements: Security Awareness, Medical First Aid updates, ECDIS training, Electro-Technical Officers (ETO), rest hours enforcement. |
| 4 | Flag State, Port State, and STCW | Flag State issues and endorses certificates. Port State Control (PSC) checks them. What "endorsement of recognition" means for a Panamanian officer serving on a Liberian-flagged ship. |

---

### Season 2 — Basic Safety Training (order: 2)
> Context: The baseline training every seafarer must complete before setting foot on a ship. STCW Regulation VI/1.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal Survival Techniques (VI/1-1) | Lifeboat and liferaft survival: boarding, signaling, rationing, hypothermia prevention. The STCW competency table A-VI/1-1 explained. AMP course: BST Técnicas de Supervivencia. |
| 2 | Fire Prevention and Firefighting (VI/1-2) | Fire types (A/B/C/D/F), extinguisher selection, breathing apparatus donning, boundary cooling. STCW table A-VI/1-2. AMP course: Lucha Contra Incendios. |
| 3 | Elementary First Aid (VI/1-3) | CPR, bleeding control, fracture immobilization, burns, shock management. The difference between Elementary First Aid (BST) and Medical First Aid (advanced). |
| 4 | Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (VI/1-4) | Shipboard safety culture, fatigue awareness, drugs and alcohol policy, harassment and bullying, environmental responsibility. The "soft skills" of STCW. |
| 5 | BST Revalidation — The 5-Year Rule | When BST expires, what revalidation requires (refresher course or sea service proof), and why letting it lapse can ground your certificate. |

---

### Season 3 — Watchkeeping Standards (order: 3)
> Context: STCW Chapter VIII defines the legal minimum standards for watchkeeping on the bridge and in the engine room.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chapter VIII — Watchkeeping Principles | The duty to maintain a proper watch. What constitutes "proper look-out" under STCW (and COLREGS Rule 5). Responsibilities of the OOW while on watch. |
| 2 | Minimum Rest Hours | STCW/MLC requirement: 10 hours rest in any 24-hour period, 77 hours in any 7-day period. How rest hour records are kept, what PSC looks for, and why falsification is common and dangerous. |
| 3 | Fit for Duty | The legal obligation to report for watch fit to perform duties. Alcohol limits (0.05% blood alcohol in most flag states). Prescription medication and performance. The officer's right and duty to refuse an unfit watch. |
| 4 | Handover of the Watch | The minimum information that must be exchanged at every watch handover per STCW (position, course, speed, traffic, weather, outstanding orders). The 5-minute overlap recommendation. |

---

### Season 4 — Certification, Endorsements & Revalidation (order: 4)
> Context: The administrative reality of maintaining valid certificates — what expires, what needs renewal, and what fraud looks like.

| # | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Certificate of Competency (CoC) vs. Endorsement | The Flag State CoC is the officer's qualification. The Endorsement of Recognition (EoR) is issued by the Flag State of the ship to accept a foreign CoC. Two separate documents, both required when serving on foreign-flag ships. |
| 2 | Revalidation — The 5-Year Cycle | What must be renewed every 5 years: CoC, GMDSS, medical fitness, proficiency updates. The difference between revalidation by sea service (12 months in past 5 years) vs. refresher course. |
| 3 | Panama AMP Certificates — Practical Guide | How Panama AMP issues and registers CoCs. The AMP online verification portal. What STCW endorsements look like on an AMP certificate and why foreign companies verify them. |
| 4 | Certificate Fraud and IMO GISIS Verification | How fraudulent certificates are detected: IMO GISIS database, AMP online registry, white-list countries. The career and legal consequences of a fraudulent certificate. Leto's Verified Badge in the context of employer trust. |
